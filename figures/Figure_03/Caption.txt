Fig. 3 | Retrospective selective grinding policies across measurement burdens. Panels a–d show crude protein, moisture, crude ash and crude fibre. Blue circles and solid lines indicate disagreement-guided routing, orange squares and dashed lines indicate matched random routing, and gray triangles and dotted lines indicate a hindsight oracle. Guided routing ranks the standard deviation of predictions across eight whole grain model families within each outer test fold, then replaces selected whole grain predictions with the corresponding direct ground grain predictions. Requested fractions of 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 30%, 50% and 100% are plotted at their realized sample fractions after integer quota allocation; the requested 10% and 20% settings route 10/98 and 20/98 samples, respectively. Every point is the median RMSE across ten outer repeats. Shaded bands span the 25th–75th percentiles across repeats and are not confidence intervals. For the random policy, the metric in each repeat is first averaged over 500 fold-quota-matched random selections. This descriptive curve differs from the exact expected random squared-error comparator used for the inferential comparisons in the text. The oracle selects samples using their observed squared-error improvements and cannot be deployed without knowing the outcomes. Lines connect evaluated budgets for visualization and do not estimate a continuous optimum. Each policy uses the same 98 coded samples. The x axis measures sample routing burden only and excludes calibration measurements, labour, time and monetary costs. The panels use different y-axis scales. RMSE is expressed in percentage points.
Reference concentrations and their predictions are expressed on an as-is (wet) basis. Concentration-error metrics are in percentage points of that basis.
